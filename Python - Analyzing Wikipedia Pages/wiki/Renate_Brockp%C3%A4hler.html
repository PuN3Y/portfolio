From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
German folklorist

Renate Brockpähler (9 March 1927 − 23 November 1989)[1] was a German folklorist.

Life and career

[edit]

Brockpähler was the daughter of the writer and local historian Wilhelm Brockpähler [de] and the elder sister of the writer Wolfgang Brockpähler [de]. From 1944 she was in the Reich Labour Service and until March 1945 in the camp Gorspen-Vahlsen [de], where she got to know the meaning of singing.

In 1959 she was awarded the title of Dr. phil. with a thesis on the history of baroque opera in Germany at the Westfälische Wilhelms-Universität. She was a member of the Kommission Alltagskulturforschung für Westfalen [de][2] and head of the Westfälisches Volksliedarchiv [de] in Münster. Brockpähler published several articles in the Rheinisch-westfälische Zeitschrift für Volkskunde.[3]

With Dietmar Sauermann [de] she worked on a prisoner of war project, during which she died at the age of 62.[4]
