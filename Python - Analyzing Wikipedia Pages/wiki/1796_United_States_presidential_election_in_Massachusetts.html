A presidential election was held in Massachusetts on November 7, 1796, as part of the 1796 United States presidential election.[1] The Federalist vice president John Adams defeated the Democratic-Republican former secretary of state Thomas Jefferson.[2]

Federalist electors carried seven electoral districts with a majority of the popular vote.[3] The Massachusetts General Court subsequently chose Federalists to represent the seven remaining districts, and appointed two additional electors to represent the state at-large. The electors voted for Adams as a block, but split their second votes among three other candidates.[4] Divisions among Northern Federalists allowed Jefferson to succeed Adams as vice president under the rule which awarded the office to the runner-up in the presidential election.[5]
