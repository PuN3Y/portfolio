Aplysia sydneyensis, the sydney seahare is a species of gastropod mollusc in the genus Aplysia, family Aplysiidae.[1] It is often found in the intertidal zone on the Australian coast.[2]

Description

[edit]

The colour of the sydney seahare varies, and individuals can be near-black, brown, red or yellow.[3] It is covered in spots that vary from off white to dark brown, and has a black lined pattern on the head and sides. The head is small, and the rhinophores are slender and close together.[4] It grows to approx 150mm in length.[2] When startled, this species can produce a cloud of dark purple ink.[2]

Aplysia sydneyensis has a small, thin and flat shell, typically hidden by the parapodia (mantle flaps).[5] Shell coloration is chestnut at the median region, becoming pale horn-colored toward the margins. The apex is obtuse, terminal, slightly reflected and incurved. The dorsal margin is convex; the upper margin is straight; the outer lip is straight; the lower margin is subquadrate.[6]

Life history

[edit]

Copulation and egg-laying takes place between April through to June.[7] A. sydneyensis feeds on algae such as Laurencia obtusa.[8]

Distribution

[edit]

This marine species is endemic to Australia and occurs off New South Wales, Queensland, South Australia, Tasmania, Victoria and Western Australia.
