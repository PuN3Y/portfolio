Shahriar Pourdanesh (born July 19, 1970) is a former American football offensive lineman who played in the National Football League (NFL) for the Washington Redskins, Pittsburgh Steelers, and Oakland Raiders from 1996 to 2001 when in 2001 a severe knee injury cut his career short.

He is distinguished as being the first Iranian to play in the National Football League (NFL).[1]

Early life

[edit]

Pourdanesh was born on July 19, 1970 in Tehran, but left at age 9 for Germany, while trying to gain entry to the United States. They gained this permission in 1983.[2]

Pourdanesh is a graduate of University High School in Irvine, California.

An All-American and All Conference selection, and two time team captain for the University of Nevada at Reno, where in 1992 he was awarded Nevada's Most Outstanding Offensive Player award.[2] He was an integral part and leader of one of the nation's top offenses during that span, and helped lead the Wolfpack to three conference titles and a spot in the 1990 1AA National Title game. He was inducted into the Nevada athletic Hall of Fame in 2005.

Professional career

[edit]

He previously played with the Baltimore Stallions of the Canadian Football League from 1994 to 1995, where he was an All-Star and won the CFL's Most Outstanding Offensive Lineman Award in 1994 (the first ever rookie to be honored so), and was a member of the Grey Cup Championship team in 1995. He was also signed to a one-year deal with the Cleveland Browns in 1993 under coach Bill Belichick. He played for the Washington Redskins before being traded to Pittsburgh in August 1999 for a conditional seventh-round selection in the 2000 draft.[3] He is the only Iranian-born football player in NFL history and has over 80 professional starts in his career.

Personal life

[edit]

Shar and his family escaped the Iranian Revolution in 1979 and fled to Europe. The Pourdanesh family settled in Irvine, California in 1983 after spending 3 years in West Germany.
