Tuxedo is an unincorporated community in southern Henderson County, North Carolina, United States, which was originally called Lakewood.

Etymology

[edit]

Early in the 20th century, the newly formed village of Lakewood, North Carolina, changed its name to Tuxedo (after a town in NY State) to avoid confusion with a similarly named town in North Carolina.[3][4] Tuxedo is claimed to be a corruption of an Indian phrase of unknown origin, p'tauk suttough, supposedly meaning "place of the bears."[5] The name of Tuxedo, New York, however, originally derives from the Lenape word ptukwsiit (Munsee dialect) or tùkwsit (Unami dialect), meaning "round foot" or Wolf Clan.[6]

History

[edit]

A large mill, the Green River Manufacturing plant, employed some 250 area workers after its completion in 1910.[3] That same year, the post office was renamed Tuxedo (after having used Lakewood name since its 1908 establishment), and remained in operation until 1993.[7] The mill, at the time called WestPoint Pepperell–Tuxedo, closed for good in 1990.[3]

Camp Arrowhead, located in Tuxedo, was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2005.[8] Tuxedo is also home to camps Mondamin, Green Cove, Talisman, Greystone and Falling Creek Camp.

Location

[edit]

Tuxedo is located on U.S. Route 25 and U.S. Route 176, approximately 9 miles (14 km) north of the South Carolina border, at 2,054 feet (626 m) above sea level. It is home to North Carolina's Green River Nature Preserve.[3]

Nearby Lake Summit was formed in 1920 by an impoundment of the Green River when additional power was needed for area businesses in the past.[3]
